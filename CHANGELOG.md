# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Every session and every pause can be read back.** The session list and the roadmap's revisions returned their newest hundred and no more, and the owner's pause kept only its latest word, so nobody (an auditor testing a period, Evidence Desk collecting one) could read a project's whole record. Both lists now page (`before`, and `next` when there is more), and every request of the owner's word (an org's on each of its projects too, a repeat marked `unchanged`, what an account already held entered once as `backfilled`) and every change of state the automation reported is kept and served newest first at `GET /v1/accounts/:account/state/history` behind the `overview` panel; an org's own history is its steer key's alone (ADR 0003, amended). A reason is redacted before it is kept, and an account id holding `:` is refused on every read, which could otherwise name another account's records. SDK 3.5.0 (`sessions(account, limit, before)`, `stateHistory`, `roadmapRevisionPage`), kit 3.9.0 (vendors it; this repository's and the cookbook's vendored client had lagged the SDK since 3.3.0 and now match it). The kit's setup guide no longer names a docs page a new project does not have yet, which failed the docs check on main.
- **An org's key reads its projects** (owner's ruling). The org's steer key, minted only through `<org>/.github`, now reads every project of the org as that project's own key does, on every door, and `GET /v1/orgs/:org` (so `oa status <org>`) lists every project of the org with every figure. Anyone else still sees what everyone may see. On the Volter tenant, where every project is private, the org's rollup was empty for the org's own owner.
- **The kit takes orchestrator 0.3.6.** `hermes cron status` reads an orchestrator-run home as a live scheduler (the orchestrator writes Hermes's ticker heartbeat), and a fire that went silent past its limit is a durable incident, as under Hermes. Both were found by the company pilot's own agent in its report.
- **The kit takes orchestrator 0.3.5.** A Codex worker now holds the profile's `agent.max_turns` (the orchestrator counts each turn's model calls and interrupts past the cap; Codex takes no cap at launch), so a turn that runs away on a denial ends there, as a Claude Code worker's does. A home whose model the owner set by hand to what the declaration now says starts without a false conflict. Measured on the company pilot: a scheduled fire on a Codex worker through the owner's subscription completed, mirrored into Hermes's session store and published, at no metered cost.
- **Money travels with the books on every door.** A door the owner opened wider than the books (the sessions, a transcript, the calls, an item, the account's event stream) carried the money anyway: each session's and call's cost, an item's cost, and the balance, spend and grants in the stream's pulse. For a viewer the owner keeps from the books, every such record now comes without its `*usd_cents` figures or which funder's envelope paid (`envelope`, `envelopes`, `gift_id`), on the API and the dashboard's session page alike, and the `now` badge, which a shared cache keeps for everyone, shows a live run's cost only when the books are open to everyone; the project's own key and the admitted roster still see them. `oa status` on a project whose books are closed reads the rest instead of reporting no such project, and `oa` prints a withheld figure as —. A gift the platform refused comes back on the project it was meant for, not the first in the list. SDK: the cost fields of sessions, items, calls and a session's status event are optional.
- **A closed project is not announced where everyone looks.** The org rollup (`GET /v1/orgs/:org`) served every listed project's balance, burn, runway and live sessions to anyone, including projects whose owner keeps them private; on a Volter tenant, where `/v1` bypasses Access, that listed a client's private projects with their money within minutes of going live, and the tenant was rolled back. A project is now listed (the front page, an owner's page, the rollup) only when its overview is open to everyone, its money appears only when its books are, and its live sessions only when its sessions are. The front page's and an owner's totals count only books open to everyone; a card whose books are closed shows no money and is never called unfunded or spent out, and sorting by the bank or the runway puts it last. A project's own page and dashboard follow the same rule: a viewer the owner keeps from the books gets no balance, runway, spend chart or session cost, neither drawn nor carried in the page. Before, the front page listed a private project's card with its balance, and a project on the `status` preset showed its balance and spend on its own page to everyone. A giver sent to a project not listed publicly (a team member giving to their private project) keeps it selected on the give form instead of falling to the first listed project.
- **An org has its own word, and its projects inherit it** (ADR 0010). The owner asked about an org as often as about a project, and the platform only knew projects. As cloud CLIs do it, the org's word now lives on the org (`@<owner>`, the account that already stands for the name) and the platform applies it: a project is paused when it or its org is paused, `GET /v1/accounts/:account/state` serves that effective word (`desired.from` names the org, the project's own record is beside it as `own`), and the page and dashboard say "Paused by volter-ai". Every automation that reads the state obeys an org pause with no change. The org's steer key is minted through `<org>/.github`, the repository GitHub reads as the org's own; its spend limits are `spend.limits` in that repository's `.open-autonomy/config.yaml`, synced like a project's and held in the reserve against every project of the org together, and a refusal names the org. `GET /v1/orgs/:org` shows an org at a glance. `oa` picks its scope like `gh` and `gcloud`: the argument (`org` or `org/project`), `--project`/`--org`, the checkout's remote, then `oa use`; `oa status <org>` rolls the org up, `oa pause <org>` and `oa resume <org>` set its word, `oa key mint <org>` mints its key, and an act refuses a key for another account than the one named. An org's total over its projects is enforced always but published only when every project of the org opens its books; otherwise the limit shows with its total withheld. An org's give key, which spends the org's credits, now needs the same `<org>/.github` claim: before, a claim in any repository of the org minted it. SDK 3.3.0 (`OpenAutonomy.org`, `orgMint`, `OrgView`, `OrgSpendBound`), CLI 0.2.0.
- **The default profile runs without command approvals.** Owner's decision, 2026-09-24: the IR compiles the default profile to Hermes's `approvals.mode: off`, `approvals.cron_mode: approve` and `approvals.single_query_mode: approve`, in every skew's `agent.json`. Under Hermes's defaults a command its classifier calls dangerous (a heredoc script, `python -c`, a force push) was denied in every unattended run: 174 of Hookline's 3,042 tool calls. A stock worker (Claude Code, Codex) has no `execute_code` to route around that, so the same default stopped the orchestrator's first pilot. The orchestrator reads the same keys, so both runtimes behave alike. Under them Hermes applies only its hardline blocklist, its sudo-stdin guard and any `approvals.deny` rules: the dangerous-pattern check and the security scanner (Tirith) no longer run for the default profile. The approve modes also approve a plugin's pre-tool-call requests in unattended runs. Without the gate the agent can change anything its OS user can, so a bare installation runs it as a separate user (ADR 0001); the kit's SETUP.md says so. The treasurer, which pays, keeps the gated defaults.
- **Kit 3.1.0: upgrade is a three-way merge.** A project is a branch of its skew (ADR 0006), so `upgrade` no longer rewrites the kit-owned files: it merges the kit's change into the project's copy, with the render of the version the project last took as the ancestor, fetched once from the registry into `~/.cache/open-autonomy/kit/<version>/` and rendered by that version's own engine. A file only the project changed keeps the project's; a file only the kit changed takes the kit's whole; a file both changed merges line by line, and where the same lines moved apart the conflict stays in the file, marked, for an agent in the project's own session to resolve (`maintain.ts upgrade` holds the worktree and resumes after). A file the kit retired goes if the project left it alone and stays if it changed it. `check` reports the version, the files the project changed and any unresolved merge, and exits 1 only behind the kit or mid-merge; the runtime still refuses to ship host code the project changed. `kit.json`'s `divergences` list is gone: divergence is every file's right. A kit record's version is a release number (x.y.z) and nothing else, since it names the ancestor fetched from the registry. The SDK README the setup guide linked was never vendored, so the link now points at the repository. `maintain.ts upgrade` lands the upgrade the way the repository lands changes: a `land/kit-<version>` branch where a landing workflow takes it, main itself where none stands, decided from main before the upgrade runs and with the upgrade rebased onto main as it then stands, so a manage-project repository that takes direct pushes no longer strands its upgrade on a branch nobody opens; its verification is the kit's own `check` (plus the typecheck a self-build repository ships), not a `bun run check` a hand-built project need not have. `create-open-autonomy upgrade --fleet <fleet.json>` takes every project of a fleet onto this kit from this kit: each cloned fresh, upgraded, committed and landed the same way, a project left in conflict kept unpushed with its clone. This repository's own install and the cookbook take 3.1.0's files.
- **Kit 3.0.0: the kit is a lineage.** `template/` is `base/` plus three skews, `self-build`, `manage-project` and `manage-organization`, laid over it whole-file (ADR 0006); `create` and `adopt` take `--skew`, `kit.json` records it, and a record older than the skews is self-build. The self-build PM no longer reads a constitution to learn whether it may dispatch. The manage-project PM keeps a hand-built project's roadmap and changelog true once a day, asks the owners of stalled work and proposes releases, with no board. The manage-organization agent gathers an organization's projects daily, posts the memo with the agenda, records the meeting's outcomes and files them down as requests. The host package under `.open-autonomy/` is the same bytes in every project. Several projects run together as a fleet: `start.ts --fleet` composes one executor's multiplexed Hermes home from each project's own `hermes/`, one valve with a key and a GitHub App per project (the valve takes `--github-app` repeatably), one reporter per project; `create-open-autonomy fleet` writes the definition and the executor's World with one volume per checkout, whose mounts and limits the executor now takes from its definition. A slim executor image for the daily PM skews (`container/Dockerfile.slim`, 0.98 GB against 2.84 GB) whose plugin denylist a synced home keeps; the community door creates a Discussion (`discussion-new`), the organization skill's posting act.
- **The kit record keeps itself in step.** Correcting the number once was not enough: the next kit release drifted it again within the hour. A pre-commit hook in `.githooks` sets `.open-autonomy/kit.json` to the version in `packages/kit-hermes` and stages the correction, so this repository's page cannot report a kit nobody is running. Enable it with `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
- **The health door tells the truth.** `/healthz` answered `ok` with a 200 through a twelve-minute Durable Object outage on 2026-09-15, while every page and every read door returned 500: it never touched the store. It now reads the funding account's pulse under a two-second bound, reports what the store did, and answers 503 when the store cannot answer.
- **This repository's own kit record says what it runs.** `.open-autonomy/kit.json` still read 2.11.1 while the boilerplate beside it was the 2.11.17 template, so our own project page reported a kit version nobody was running. The record tracks the kit this repository publishes.
- **A permanently diverged session settles instead of retrying forever.** Hermes rewrites a long run's earlier turns when it compresses, and the platform is append-only, so such a session could never be reconciled: our own reporter was reloading 33 of them from Supercode every minute, some first published on 6 September. When the platform's record is no longer live there is nothing left to publish, so the reporter settles at its checkpoint and stops. Kit 2.11.18.
- **A generated project gets the SDK the kit was built with.** Every kit published today pinned `@open-autonomy/sdk` at 3.0.0 while the workspace carried 3.1.0, so `create-open-autonomy upgrade` vendored a client that sends no key on reads. A project whose page is not open then read its own sessions as `not_open`, and its reporter's orphan sweep threw on every pass. The release now pins a workspace dependency to the version in the checkout instead of leaving it to the publisher, and the kit refuses to vendor an SDK below the version it needs. Kit 2.11.17.
- **The owner's pause covers the board.** Pausing stopped the scheduled desks but not the board dispatcher, which went on starting queued tasks; a paused agent ran a developer task for half an hour. On pause the reporter now defers every task waiting to be picked up (a run in flight still finishes) and remembers them; resume promotes exactly those. The observed note says how many were deferred. Kit 2.11.16.
- **A task branch is not a moved main.** The start script judged "main moved" by comparing HEAD with origin/main, so a developer run that checked out its task branch in the project checkout made the stack drain and restart under it, twice. It now compares origin/main with what the stack started on. Kit 2.11.16.
- **The reviewer has a named door for its verdict.** `community.ts review <pr> approve|request-changes <full sha> <evidence…>` submits the GitHub review as the project's own App, pinned to the exact head reviewed; the develop skill names it as the one review-submission path. An Evidence Desk reviewer, told only to use "the project's configured GitHub door", found no command for it and blocked a finished, verified PR on "review capability". Kit 2.11.15.
- **Setup turns auto-merge on.** The landing workflow arms GitHub's auto-merge on every pull request it opens and lets the required approval release it; a repository without auto-merge lets no landing through once main requires that approval. Hookline, set up before the kit had a setup step, had it off, a main that required no approval, and an App that could not review at all. The kit's setup now enables auto-merge on the repository it prepares; the ruleset and the App manifest already asked for the rest. Kit 2.11.14.
- **A failed publication waits a minute.** Every publication attempt reloads a session's whole history from Supercode; a live session whose earlier turns Hermes rewrote (compression) cannot be appended to until it ends, and the reporter retried it every tick: 73 full reloads over one twelve-minute PM run. A failed key now waits a minute before the next attempt. Kit 2.11.13.
- **The agent's browser is its own.** Hermes's browser-use harness attaches to a Chrome already running on the host, which on a shared machine is a person's: an Evidence Desk developer met the owner's remote-debugging permission prompt mid-task (and declined it). The kit's Hermes configuration now disables that toolset; the browser tools that spawn an isolated headless browser stay. Kit 2.11.12.
- **A restarting reporter waits for the native ledger instead of dying.** On a restart onto a moved main, the gateway that just drained can still be writing its run ledger when the new reporter first reads it; the reporter threw "Native run ledger unreadable" and took the whole stack down for a second start. It now retries for up to a minute. Kit 2.11.11.
- **Bare mode gives the agent what the container gave it.** The kit's own tools, the World CLI among them, are on the agent's PATH, and `OPEN_AUTONOMY_DATA` names a data root beside its home, outside the checkout, for its verification World and scratch (the container mounts the same at `/opt/data`). A project moved from a container to a bare host found `volter-world` missing and its World instructions pointing at the retired container. Kit 2.11.10.
- **One task per outcome, whatever its size (owner ruling, 2026-09-13).** The PM desk used to cut a roadmap outcome into work keys: a CLI report here, a follow-up there, each a task with a fresh context that had to rebuild what the last one knew, and the pieces never quite met. A task is now the whole arc of its outcome: one development stream holds every piece of context the outcome needs and hands over a finished outcome. The queue helper takes an outcome, a title and a body, keys the task on the outcome alone, and hands back the outcome's existing task instead of creating a second; only a cancelled task makes room. Distinct workstreams are distinct roadmap outcomes. The PM and strategy skills say why. Kit 2.11.9.
- **A stopped agent leaves after its gateway, not before.** The kit's start script answered SIGTERM by signalling its children and exiting 300 ms later; launchd started the successor at once, whose gateway found the old one still winding down ("already running") and died, costing a second start. The script now waits for every child to exit (fifteen seconds, then SIGKILL) before it leaves. Kit 2.11.8.
- **A restarted reporter leaves finished sessions alone.** The kit's reporter used to reload every session it had ever published (all of its Supercode history, then the platform's copy) each time it started, ~200 reads for a project a few days old, to find nothing to add. A session whose checkpoint already carries its end is done: the reporter now marks it stopped without a read, and the publisher answers from the checkpoint. Kit 2.11.7.
- **A running agent follows its main without anyone on the host.** The start script now fetches the checkout's main every ten minutes; when it moved and the board is quiet, the stack drains and restarts onto it, the path a kit upgrade already took through `kit-restart.json`. A landed change of any kind (the config, the seeds, the kit) reaches every running agent within the hour, and no upgrade needs a service-manager restart by hand.
- **Empty books no longer call a running agent "not yet funded".** A project whose model is its owner's own provider (the setup names it) runs on no platform funds, so its pages and `oa status` said "Not yet funded" while it worked. The books decide the standing only when the model is the platform's rail; otherwise the automation's own reported state does.
- **The community desk wakes on change, not on the clock.** Every project's community job was a plain model run every quarter hour that mostly found nothing. It is now a Hermes monitor job: every five minutes the kit's `community-monitor.sh` prints when the newest issue, pull request or discussion last changed (`community.ts latest`, stable bytes, no cursor touched), and the scheduler wakes the agent only when that output changed, handing it the diff. This repository's own seed, the template's, Hookline's and Evidence Desk's all carry it; a project's seed is its own, so an existing project changes its `cron/jobs.seed.json` the same way.
- **`oa`: Open Autonomy on the command line.** A new package, `@open-autonomy/cli`, puts the platform's doors on the command line as the SDK speaks them: `oa status` (the word, the money, what is running, the board), `oa sessions` and `oa session <key> --follow` (a transcript, followed while live), `oa roadmap`, `oa books --calls`, the owner's `oa pause` and `oa resume` on a steer key (recorded, then the automation's answer awaited), and `oa key mint --scopes steer` / `oa key rotate`, the kit's claim-and-mint flow outside a checkout. A read needs no key for what the owner opened to everyone; a closed panel opens to the project's own key, read from `~/.config/open-autonomy/<owner>/<project>/` when no `--key` is given. `--json` prints the records. The SDK client (3.1.0) gained `funding()`, `calls()` and `follow()`, and its reads carry the key when there is one.
- **A home whose model still points at the old custom Codex provider is told so at start.** Since the kit forwards Hermes's native `openai-codex` provider through the valve, a config that names a custom provider at `HERMES_CODEX_BASE_URL` gets no valve and an unexpanded address, and every run fails on a connection error with nothing said; our own agent lost a night of runs to it after this morning's upgrade. The start script now names the misconfiguration in its log, and this repository's own config uses the native provider.
- **The kit's version is read from its manifest.** The version was also written into the kit's source, so the 2.11.2 release still called itself 2.11.1: an upgraded project recorded the old number and the PM's maintenance would have kept offering the same upgrade. 2.11.3 reads the one number from package.json.
- **The reporter reads native state every thirty seconds, not every tick.** Each read of the harness's orchestration and run ledger is a second or two of disk, and a tick came every five seconds and on every session event, so reads piled up and one in ten stalled past the thirty-second limit, leaving that tick unpublished. The read now happens at most every thirty seconds (or right after a failure), is given a minute, and retries once when Hermes's atomic rewrite of a file leaves its name missing for an instant.
- **A session Hermes never closed is ended, not left live.** A run killed under Hermes leaves a native session with no end, and the reporter used to end a session only from a native end, so it stayed live on the platform forever and the page said "Working now" for a session two days silent. Now a native session not proven running by the host and silent for six hours is ended at its last message, with no outcome, since none was recorded; and a platform-live session absent from native discovery for five minutes is ended at its last turn.
- **The owner's word on visibility holds on the SDK's read doors, not only on the pages.** `GET /v1/accounts/:account/sessions`, a session and its event stream, the account's event stream, the roadmap, an item, the metered calls, the funding figures and the widgets answer according to the project's `dashboard:` visibility; a panel closed to the public is 404 unless the request carries the project's own key or a signed-in viewer the roster admits. Under the default `roadmap` preset that closes the session list, transcripts and calls to the public; the now widget and the operating state stay at the overview's level. The dashboard's overview and the landing page, likewise, carry a session's identity and standing to whoever sees the overview and its report only to whoever sees sessions.
- **The landing page and the dashboard are two surfaces, and the dashboard is built from Supercode's UI kit.** `/owner/project` is the platform's landing page for an outsider: Product Hunt's top (the icon, the name, the one line, the tags, two buttons) over Kickstarter's campaign (a poster of the agent at work with its metered days, the money and the runway against the goal, the story, the roadmap as one bar, the patrons, the tiers); no cover band. `/owner/project/dashboard` and beneath (`sessions[/key]`, `board[/item]`, `books`, `agent`, `team`) is the work as the team reads it, composed from `@volter-ai-dev/supercode-ui`: the kit's session inventory and conversation, its workflow board, its job details, runs and pause (the owner's one control); the SDK's records are projected into the kit's models, the worker renders the tree and the browser hydrates it from `/assets/dashboard.js` (built by a deployment's `bun run build`, served as Workers assets), and a live session's turns land as they are narrated. A self-host has no landing page and serves the dashboard at the project's address. The earlier tabbed project page, its slots and its parts are gone; the owner's word on visibility gates every dashboard address as before.
- **Direction in a coding session is verified direction.** The communication agreement names the channel it had left out: what the owner says in a coding session on their own machine, under their own login, is direction from that login, with the session as its link and the record quoting its scope, never its contents. The Discord rule (verify the author's ID against the roster) stays for public channels. ADR 0004 (local Worlds use actual capacity and backend limits) is recorded on that footing, as ADRs 0003 and 0005 already were.
- **The project page is a dashboard; the campaign is the platform's.** The core's page keeps a compact header, the workshop, the board, the books and the team; money on it is a budget someone put in. The cover, the pitch, the patrons wall and the ask enter through the platform's slots (`cover`, `lead`, `side`), so a self-host carries none of them. **By default the public sees the roadmap and the books**, and the team sees the sessions, the transcripts and the agent; the `dashboard:` block's `roadmap` preset is what an absent block means, `open` opens everything.
- **A finished session is never called live.** When the platform's retained transcript no longer matches the native one, the kit's reporter used to refuse the session entirely, so a session that had ended natively stayed live on the books for good. It now publishes the end over the transcript as it stands and rewrites nothing. On the page, a session in flight outranks the books: an agent on its owner's own subscription says working, not unfunded. The top bar no longer wraps at phone width. Kit 2.11.1.
- **The kit ships the runtime, and the runtime says what it is through the SDK.** `create-open-autonomy runtime <dir>` materializes a real installation's host runtime from a landed checkout, outside the agent-writable tree: a release of the kit with its dependencies, the World definition whose executor lifecycle is the kit's own `container/executor.ts` (one container on kept home and checkout volumes, an optional provider to resume; it never creates volumes or images, so an empty home can never become a new agent with the old name), the image build definition, and the launchd unit that runs World with `start.ts --container` in the foreground. Run it again after `upgrade` lands and restart through the service manager. The setup event gains `runtime` (`mode`, `kit`, `executor`, `host`): the start script tells the reporter what runs the agent, the page's Agent tab shows it. The template's hand-copied `container/world.json` and `build-world.json` are gone; the verb writes them. Kit 2.11.0 ([PR #646](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/646)).
- **A settled Polar patron returns to the wall without entering recurring accrual.** A successful non-idempotent Polar order records the patron through the platform extension after the mint, while the recurring sponsor list remains separate so the same purchase is not minted again ([PR #651](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/651)).
- **Production secret sync now carries the signed-in giving door.** The existing `sync-secrets` operation maps GitHub-compatible production-environment aliases onto the Worker names for Sponsors and OAuth, and installs the giving session HMAC secret when present; provisioning and running the operation remain human production actions ([PR #652](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/652)).
- **The pages are GitHub's addresses over the books, and the core serves them.** `/` is the deployment's front, `/name` a login's page (an org's projects and grants pool, a person's giving), `/owner/project` a project with `work`, `sessions`, `books`, `agent`, `team` and `about` beneath it; `/p/…` is gone. The core's pages know givers, the roster and everyone else; the platform's patronage (Explore's pitch and patrons, the tiers, the patrons wall, buying credits, sponsoring) enters through slots per page, and a self-host carries none of it. The owner's `dashboard:` block in `.open-autonomy/config.yaml` (a preset, then a role per panel) decides who sees what. The kit now includes that block as its generated example, and the page router asks the app for viewer identity only on addresses it serves ([PR #649](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/649)). The workshop and a transcript follow a live session in place; the page reloads only when a session starts or ends, the roadmap revision changes or the owner's word changes ([PR #639](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/639)). Who is looking comes from an app's identity door: on the platform the GitHub sign-in names the viewer, and a project's own records say what that login is to it (owner or team from the committed roster, giver from the books); the owner, signed in, pauses or resumes the agent from the Agent tab. A self-host has no door and serves everyone the public view, so a panel set past `public` there is closed to all ([PR #640](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/640)).
- **Project pages can be designed without changing the served site.** A new `apps/storybook` renders the proposed project overview and tabs, deployment directory and GitHub-name account pages from captured public records. The reusable backend owns the core pages while the platform adds patronage, Explore and account actions through typed slots; the same modules serve the site ([PR #629](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/629), [PR #633](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/633), [PR #634](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/634)).
- **Exhausted funds are not an owner pause.** A funded balance spent to zero is now `exhausted`, and the runway widget says spending stopped; `paused` remains the owner's operating-state word ([PR #618](https://github.com/open-autonomy-org/open-autonomy/pull/618)).
- **One door for everything the automation says.** The timeline travels on the events door (`org.open-autonomy.timeline`, `timeline()`) like sessions, tasks, setup and the operating state; `POST /v1/agent/roadmap` is the owner's steer door alone, and the valve no longer forwards it. Every client write answers `{ ok, status, error? }`.
- **An update has an identity.** A publisher that gives an update event an `id` can send it again after a lost acknowledgement or a restart and get the same record back, `idempotent`; the kit's reporter uses its note's key as that id.
- **The board is not a shape the platform knows.** The `item.task` event (lanes, attempts, review verdicts, handoffs) is gone: the lane is the item's status, the attempts are its sessions, and the kit's reporter publishes each review verdict and handoff once as an update on the item. The item page's board panel went with it.
- **The SDK is the interface and nothing else** ([ADR 0005](docs/decisions/0005-the-sdk-is-the-interface.md)). `@open-autonomy/sdk` 3.0 keeps the client, the timeline codec and drivers, the roster model and the key helpers, and depends on nothing. The host tools moved to the kit as its own files under `.open-autonomy/` (`valve.ts`, `credentials.ts`, `codex-auth.ts`, `reporting.ts`), the owner's config parsers to the backend; the `open-autonomy-valve` and `open-autonomy-credentials` binaries are gone, run them with Bun from the checkout.
- **The owner's running/paused word travels through the SDK** ([ADR 0003](docs/decisions/0003-operating-state-through-the-sdk.md)). `POST /v1/agent/state` on a steer key records the owner's request; the automation reads `GET /v1/accounts/:account/state`, applies it its own way and reports `org.open-autonomy.agent.state` once true; the page shows the request beside the answer.
